Module Design & Delivery, ENVM2002/7002 Marine and Coastal Environmental Protection (2023–2025)
Iteratively designed and developed a new course module (three weeks of lectures per year, four weeks prepared overall), delivered in an interactive lectorial style: The Anthropocene Ocean (2023, 2024), Aquatic Food System Impacts (2023–2025), Calculating Cumulative Impacts (2023–2025), and Impact Mitigation and Offsets in the Marine Environment (2025).
Designed and ran the course field trip, including an online alternative for external students (2023–2025). Field Trip Coordinator from 2023.

Individual teaching evaluation average score 4.6/5 (2025).
Field Trip Design & Guest Lecturing, BIOL3024 Marine Wildlife Conservation (2023, 2024)

Designed and delivered a new four-hour field workshop on bird surveys and citizen science techniques for the weekend field trip to Moreton Bay Research Station.

University of Cambridge, Department of Zoology

Project Co-Supervisor (Oct 2020 – May 2021)
Supervised two final-year undergraduate students on independent projects using acoustic telemetry to investigate the movement ecology of green and hawksbill turtles in Seychelles, in collaboration with co-supervisors in the Zoology department and Save Our Seas.
Supervisor / Tutor (Apr 2018 – Oct 2022)
Part IA (first year): Evolution & Behaviour. Organised and taught small-group sessions accompanying this introduction to evolutionary biology, combining revision of course concepts, problem-based discussion and student-led inquiry. Topics included the origins of life on earth, plant evolution and the radiation of angiosperms, animal diversity and function, behavioural ecology, and global change. I also worked with first-year students on study skills, including academic writing, searching literature, reading papers, and science in society.
Part II (third year): Conservation Science. Small-group tutorials supporting lecture content on global ecosystems, biodiversity and the threats facing our planet, as well as conservation strategies and solutions and their societal and environmental impacts.
Part II (third year): Evolution & Behaviour: Populations & Societies. Small-group tutorials supporting lecture content on animal social (and eusocial) behaviour, animal personality, signalling and communication, and family interactions.
In both Part II courses, students also worked on essay structure and writing, communication, and presentation skills.
Instructor, Ecology Field Course (Jun 2018)
Collaborated with three academic staff to deliver a 10-day residential field course for second-year undergraduates, in which students designed and executed group research projects.
Wrote and delivered short sessions on ecology, R, experimental design, and statistical methods, and instructed on field methods including sampling transects, plant ID keys, and invertebrate trapping.
Lead Instructor, Introduction to R Programming, Gates Cambridge Scholars (Nov 2018)
Designed, wrote and delivered a three-hour introduction to R workshop on data manipulation and visualisation for graduate students.

University of Queensland, School of Biological Sciences

Tutor, Integrative and Physiological Zoology (2015, Jul – Dec 2016)
Guided groups of six third-year students through activities to develop practical laboratory skills and conceptual understanding of physiological systems. This final compulsory course of the Zoology major uses practical experiments (e.g. closed-system respirometry) to illustrate physiological concepts such as oxygen consumption rates.
Tutor, Global Challenges in Biology (2016)
Planned and led sessions for first-year biological science students, revising and extending course content as well as discussing study skills and exam techniques.
Course Assistant, Introduction to R Programming, UQ Postgraduate Society (2015)
Assisted the Biology department’s statistics consultant to deliver a day-long R workshop for postgraduate students.
